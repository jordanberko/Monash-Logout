Authorised Materials QC · Process

Check that authorised materials agree across the relevant exam sources and are clear enough for students and supervisors to follow.

Allow 30 to 40 minutes 12 sections 16 worked examples 14 question self-test
01

Introduction

What this process is for, and who it is written for.

Authorised materials are the items and resources a student may use during an exam, such as notes, calculators and working sheets. Quality checking (QC) confirms that the relevant sources describe the same permissions and that the instructions are clear enough to follow.

There are two ways this goes wrong.

The sources disagree, so a student prepares against one set of rules and is invigilated against another.

Or the sources agree on something too vague to enforce, so the call gets made differently in different rooms.

Both are failures this process exists to catch.

The role of QC

Our job is not to decide what should be permitted. It is to confirm that every source says the same thing, and that what they say can actually be acted on, then to raise it when either fails. A unit where every source agrees can still need a faculty decision.

Who this is for

Anyone in the Delivery Team asked to quality check authorised materials, including staff doing it for the first time. It assumes no prior knowledge of eAssessment, eVigilation or the Lookup Tool.

Read it in order the first time. After that, section 12 is the day to day reference.

What one check covers

One check is one exam shell: a single unit code, in one assessment period, for one sitting and one cohort. A unit such as ACW3120 can have an Australian shell, a Malaysian shell and an AAA shell in the same period, each with its own authorised materials, each checked separately. The shell name in col A tells you which one you have.

For that shell you produce three things, and nothing else: a category in col G, an outcome in col H, and the Comments in col I.

Where the work is recorded

Every result goes in the Authorised Materials QC 2026 sheet. That sheet is not a source. It is where you write down what the sources said.

1

You record, you do not resolve. A first QC never contacts a faculty and never edits an exam page. You describe what you found in Comments col I and set the outcome. Senior Delivery decides what is escalated and does the escalating.

2

If you are unsure, write it down. An entry in Comments costs nothing. A missed discrepancy costs a student their exam conditions. Uncertainty is a reason to record, not a reason to skip.

03

The sources we QC against

What each one is, who reads it, and how to open it.

Students read this

eAssessment

The platform students sit their exam in. The landing page carries an Instructions section and an Authorised Materials section. The most detailed of the three, because Instructions is free text.

Applies to every supervised exam
Online supervisors read this

eVigilation

The online supervision platform. Authorised materials sit behind the information icon. The fields are fixed, so wording is constrained and detail is sometimes lost.

Applies to every supervised exam
In-venue supervisors read this

Lookup Tool

The Authorised Materials Search, at authmats.evigilation.monash.edu, read on tablets at the venue. If its card is wrong, the person enforcing the rules in the room has the wrong rules.

Applies to end of semester exams only

Not a sourceThe Authorised Materials QC 2026 sheet records what the sources say. It is never evidence of what they say.

Which sources apply to your exam

Read the delivery period off the shell name in col A, not off the calendar. ACC2100 2026 Sem 1 Deferred & Supplementary is a Deferred and Supplementary exam even though it sits in the Semester 1 year, and a mid-semester assessment held during Semester 2 is not part of the Semester 2 main exam period. The period in the shell name is what decides how many sources you check.

Opening eAssessment

  1. From the QC sheet, click the link in col B. This opens the exam landing page directly and is the fastest route.

  2. If col B is blank or the link fails, go to eAssessment and search the unit code in the Search courses field.

  3. Select the correct shell. There are often several for the same unit code. Match the year, assessment period, sitting, and whether it is a main cohort, AAA or deferred shell. The shell name in col A tells you which one you want.

  4. Read both the Instructions section and the Authorised Materials section. Instructions is free text and often carries conditions that appear nowhere else, such as copy and paste restrictions or an additional device.

Opening eVigilation

  1. Open eVigilation and go to the Exam sessions tab in the left hand navigation.

  2. Set Select a date to the exam date. This is the step most often missed. eVigilation shows nothing useful until the date is right, and a unit that looks missing is usually a unit on another date.

  3. Set Search by to Exam unit, type the unit code, and search. Searching by Session browses everything running that day, which is useful when you are working through a whole date.

  4. Click the information icon next to the unit name to open the Authorised Materials panel.

  5. Read the whole panel and scroll to the bottom. It lists the category heading, then Calculator, Dictionary, Notes, Other, Permitted Item, Textbook and Working Sheets, then the Hybrid flag. A green tick means permitted, a red dash means not permitted, and the line underneath carries the detail.

Opening the Lookup Tool

End of semester exams only. Skip it entirely for every other delivery.

  1. Open the Authorised Materials Search at authmats.evigilation.monash.edu.

  2. Set the date filter to the exam date before you search. As with eVigilation, the wrong date returns nothing and looks like a missing unit.

  3. Search the unit code and open the unit card. Read the card in full.

04

Authorised material categories

The six values in the Auth Mat Category dropdown, col G.

Set the category to what the exam actually permits, not what you expect for that faculty or unit. If the exam does not fit any category cleanly, that is itself worth recording in Comments.

CategoryWhat it permitsWhat to check most carefully
Closed BookNothing beyond the standard exam environment. No notes, no calculator, no additional resources.That nothing has crept into Instructions. A Closed Book exam that mentions a calculator or a formula sheet in Instructions is not Closed Book.
Closed Book + SPIA defined, listed set of items is allowed: a physical calculator, a named virtual calculator, working sheets, or a limited number of pages of notes.Every listed item, one at a time, in every source. The most common category and the one where most discrepancies are found, because each item is a separate opportunity for the sources to disagree.
Open BookHardcopy reference materials such as textbooks, physical notes or a dictionary.Whether the materials are limited to hardcopy. Soft copy reference material is not permitted here by convention, so an Instructions section permitting digital notes is a mismatch with the category.
Open Book with Additional DeviceOpen Book plus a second device. A laptop, tablet or monitor. Not a phone. In most cases not connected to the internet.That the additional device is stated. It usually appears only in eAssessment Instructions and not in eVigilation.
Open AccessBroad access to resources during the exam.Whether generative AI is permitted. Almost all Open Access exams explicitly prohibit it. The rare exception needs flagging.
Open Access with Additional DeviceOpen Access plus a second device. Unlike Open Book with Additional Device, the second device may be connected to the internet and carry soft copy notes.That the additional device is stated, and that a supervisor reminder is flagged. Every unit in this category needs one.

Monash CollegeMC exams use three categories only: Closed Book, Closed Book + SPI, and Open Access.

07

Running a check

Pick the delivery period you are working on. The steps change by one source.

Work one exam date at a time. Filter the QC sheet by Start Time col E, sort unit names A to Z, and go down the list. Working by date keeps you aligned with how eVigilation and the Lookup Tool are searched, since both need a date set before they return anything.

  1. Confirm which rows are yours before you start. Ask Senior Delivery if you have not been told: this guide does not set out how work is allocated, and picking rows yourself risks two people checking the same shell while another is missed. Then filter the QC sheet to one exam date using Start Time col E and sort col A alphabetically within that date.

  2. Check that the row is ready and not already done. If col G, col H or col I already has something in it, stop and ask before you change it: someone may have checked this shell, and overwriting their entry loses the evidence. Then check Enrolment Status col F. Enrolled and Synced means continue. Not Enrolled means skip the unit and leave G, H and I blank. Do not record a discrepancy for a unit that is not ready. If its exam date is close and it still has not synced, see section 11.

  3. Open eAssessment from the link in col B. Confirm the shell name on the page matches col A, including the sitting and cohort. These deliveries have more shells per unit than end of semester does, so this check matters more here. A single unit can have an Australian and a Malaysian sitting, a main cohort and an AAA cohort, and a deferred shell, all in the same window.

  4. Read the Instructions section and the Authorised Materials section in full. Note every permitted item and every condition, then check the two sections agree with each other before you open anything else.

    See FIG 1 and FIG 2 in section 3.

  5. Open eVigilation and find the unit on that exam date. Open the Authorised Materials panel from the information icon, read it in full, and scroll to the bottom for the Hybrid flag.

    See FIG 3 in section 3.

  6. Open the Lookup Tool, set the date filter to the exam date, and read the unit card.

    See FIG 4 in section 3.

  7. Compare the three sourcesthe two sources field by field using section 8. Do not skim for an overall impression. Take the fields one at a time.

  8. Set Auth Mat Category col G to the category the exam actually is.

  9. Set First QC Outcome col H. All Match (eVig, eA, Lookup) is still the correct value when both sources agree, despite the name.

  10. Write what you found in Comments col I. Record anything other than a clean match, and the always-note items in section 8 even where every source agrees. Name each source and quote its wording. "eVig Notes: None. eA: one double sided A4 page permitted" is actionable. "Notes discrepancy" is not.

  11. Flag reminder units. Open Access with Additional Device, and any unit permitting generative AI. Write Gen AI in the comment so the unit is findable. See section 10.

  12. Move to the next unit. When the date is finished, check the sheet for units added after you filtered, then move to the next date.

Differences worth knowing

  • Multiple shells. Australian and Malaysian sittings of the same unit frequently differ. Check both, and compare them against each other as well as against eVigilation. A difference between the two sittings is worth recording even when each is internally consistent.
  • Late shells. These deliveries sync closer to the exam date than end of semester exams do. A unit that read Not Enrolled yesterday may be ready today. Re-check rather than assuming.
  • Unsupervised units. Units sometimes move from record only to unsupervised after the shell is built, then disappear from eVigilation. Set the outcome to Unsupervised Exam and note the date you confirmed it.
  • Monash College. Three categories only: Closed Book, Closed Book + SPI, and Open Access.
08

What to compare

Take the fields one at a time. Comparing overall impressions is how discrepancies get missed.

Sources describe the same permission in different words and the eye slides over the difference. Go field by field, in the order the eVigilation panel lists them. On the three source path, compare each field across all three: two agreeing does not settle a field the third contradicts.

Three things look alike on the page and are not the same:

What you are looking atHow to tellWhat to do
A contradictory valueTwo sources state the same field and state it differently, in a way that changes what a student brings or a supervisor allows.Discrepancy. Quote both.
Equivalent wordingDifferent words, same permission. The eVigilation dropdowns are fixed, so free text on eAssessment will rarely match word for word.Not a discrepancy. Record the wording so the next person does not re-check it.
Something a platform cannot stateThe field does not exist in that source at all, so its silence is not disagreement. An additional device and copy and paste restrictions are the common ones.Not a discrepancy. Record it, because only one source carries it.

eAssessment against itself

The Instructions section against the Authorised Materials section, on the same page.

MismatchDo this before you open anything else. Instructions is free text and is written separately from the materials list, so the two drift. A page permitting a textbook under Authorised Materials while Instructions says closed book is a discrepancy on its own, and there is no point comparing a page that already contradicts itself.

Category

eAssessment: the heading above the authorised materials list. eVigilation: the heading at the top of the panel, for example "Closed book with specifically permitted items". Lookup Tool: on the unit card.

MismatchDifferent categories in different sources. Also a category that does not match its own contents, such as a Closed Book exam listing a calculator.

Calculator

eAssessment: the Calculator line, plus any mention in Instructions. eVigilation: the Calculator field, which reads Physical Calculator, a named virtual calculator, or None.

MismatchOne source permitting a physical calculator and another a virtual one, or a named product appearing in one source only. Wording such as "faculty approved calculator" needs escalating rather than matching.

Dictionary

eAssessment: listed as a permitted item. eVigilation: the Dictionary field.

MismatchPermitted in one source and None in another. Check the platform limitations below first.

Notes

eAssessment: the Notes line and the Instructions section. eVigilation: the Notes field.

MismatchA difference in whether notes are permitted at all, whether they are physical or digital, how many pages, and whether double sided pages are allowed. Wording differences that describe the same limit are not a mismatch.

Textbook

eAssessment: listed as a permitted item. eVigilation: the Textbook field.

MismatchPermitted in one source and N/A or None in another.

Working sheets

eAssessment: usually a specific number in Instructions. eVigilation: the Working Sheets field, usually Yes or No. Lookup Tool: sometimes a number such as 99.

MismatchPermitted in one source and not another, or a different quantity in each. Compare quantities, not wording. For the Working Sheets field only: Yes, 99 and "unlimited" are the same quantity, and so are 0, None and N/A. Two different counts, or a count against unlimited, is a Discrepancy. Always record the count in Comments, match or not. These conversions belong to this field. Do not carry them across to answer sheets or to any other field.

Other and Permitted Item

eAssessment: the Other line and Instructions. eVigilation: the Other and Permitted Item fields, both free text.

MismatchAnything present in one source and absent from another. Software, Excel, formula sheets and unusual permissions all land here, so read it carefully.

Additional device

Almost always the eAssessment Instructions section only. It does not appear in the eVigilation panel.

What countsNot a mismatch when eVigilation is silent, because the field does not exist there. It is a mismatch when the category says Additional Device and no source describes what the device may be.

Generative AI

eAssessment Instructions, usually as an explicit prohibition. Occasionally as an explicit permission.

Always flagAny unit that permits generative AI needs flagging, whether or not the sources agree. This is a reminder action, not a mismatch: the sources can agree perfectly and the unit still needs flagging.

Answer sheets and the Hybrid flag

eVigilation: the Hybrid yes or no flag at the bottom of the panel. eAssessment: answer sheet instructions.

MismatchAnswer sheets are not working sheets. They belong to hybrid exams, they are counted separately, and the QC sheet treats the two as different findings. Do not compare one against the other.

The Hybrid flag is a yes or no. It tells you whether the exam has a paper component; it is not a count and it cannot agree or disagree with a number. Hybrid marked yes with no answer sheets described anywhere is a mismatch. Where both sources give an answer sheet count and the counts differ, that is a mismatch too.

Copy and paste, translation and other restrictions

eAssessment Instructions section only.

Always recordNot a source mismatch, since the other sources have nowhere to state it. Record restrictions that are difficult to enforce, and any restriction that contradicts something else on the same page.

Sitting and cohort

The shell name in col A, against the page you have open.

Check this firstIf the shell name does not match, you are reading the wrong page and nothing below it is worth comparing. Check this before anything else when something looks badly wrong.

Always note these, match or not

The comparison above decides the outcome. This list decides the comment. Each of these is worth recording in Comments even when every source agrees, because a supervisor or a student can still be caught out by it.

WhatWhat to record
Generative AIAny mention, permission or prohibition. A unit that permits it needs a supervisor reminder, so write Gen AI in the comment.
Copy and paste restrictionsUsually a paragraph after the permitted materials list. Quote it. These are hard to enforce and they get escalated.
Translation apps and websitesWhich are permitted, and on what device. Watch for a page that bans browser translation extensions and then requires a translation website.
Programming languages and softwareWhich languages, applications or packages a student may open, and where they run. Record it even when there is no discrepancy.
Handwritten notesHow many pages or sheets, what size, and whether single or double sided. “Notes permitted” without a limit is ambiguous.
CalculatorPhysical or virtual, and any restriction on type or model. “Faculty approved calculator” is not a specification.

When QC happens, when to chase, and how the second pass works.

Raise it early

There is no fixed cut-off written down. In Semester 1 2026 the first pass ran late and the second pass did not reach every unit before delivery. So the working rule is a direction, not a deadline: start early enough that both passes fit before the assessments begin, and treat an unfinished second QC column as a problem to raise rather than a normal state.

1

A unit with no First QC Outcome and its exam date approaching. Raise it with Senior Delivery rather than waiting for someone to notice.

2

A unit marked Discrepancy or All Match but Escalation Required that has not moved. A faculty needs time to answer.

3

A unit still reading Not Enrolled close to its exam date. The shell has not synced. It cannot be QC'd until it does, and someone needs to chase it.

A first QC completed the day before an exam leaves no room to escalate anything it finds, which is the same as not having done it.

RuleDetail
Every unit gets a second QCNot just the discrepancies. Every unit is checked again, independently, by a senior delivery member. There is no spot check and no sampling.

Units are added to the QC sheet after a delivery period has started. When you finish a date, go back over it for rows that were not there when you filtered. The Enrolled and Synced Shells tab and the QC Overview tab both surface units that still need a first QC.
